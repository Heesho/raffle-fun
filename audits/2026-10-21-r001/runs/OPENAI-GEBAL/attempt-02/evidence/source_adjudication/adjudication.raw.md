## Disposition

**L-01: retain the verified trust-assumption mismatch as informational, with a conditional medium economic-liveness candidate.** The permanent-refusal path is valid source reasoning under the expressly broad hostile-prize assumption. Its conditional nature does not make it false, and lack of an executable hostile-token artifact alone is not a reason to dismiss it. Nevertheless, the supplied requirements explicitly promise rollback on delivery failure and independent sponsor/treasury claims, while they do not specify a usable alternate terminal outcome after permanent prize failure. The implementation meets that rollback obligation. Treating “untrusted” as additionally promising economically successful final delivery or refund therefore requires a design interpretation. Record the mismatch and the conditional consequence without silently importing either permanent asset honesty or an unstated refund guarantee. Medium is a reasonable conditional rating if an economically usable terminal outcome for such prizes is confirmed as intended. High is not justified as an unconditional vulnerability rating from these materials.

**L-02 refinement: retain a low-severity, high-confidence source-supported violation of the explicit prohibition on clone-to-clone asset movement.** This refinement is materially stronger than the original generic other-factory/future-address concern. The destination can be a currently known same-factory clone when the protocol performs the transfer. The source omits the runtime screen on the sponsor-prize return path. An ordinary honest ERC721 suffices. Impact is limited to a prize directed by the sponsor to a badly configured future recipient, with no demonstrated theft from another sponsor or impairment of another raffle's accounted USDC.

Neither disposition is an executed result, a formal proof, a deployed-state claim, or owner acceptance.

## L-01: what is and is not established

The authoritative context calls arbitrary prize contracts untrusted. IRaffle.sol 12–15 describes supported prizes as honest standards-compliant ERC721s. Those are different trust boundaries. Factory admission checks ERC165 support at RaffleFactory.sol 171–177, performs safe escrow and verifies the asset's reported owner and raffle activation at 127–134. Raffle.sol 416–430 checks the exact expected incoming token, ID, sponsor, and factory operator. These checks establish the configured interaction and reported state at creation; they cannot bind the asset's future behavior.

For a reserve-met resolution, Raffle.sol 509–523 records NftWon. The storage-only settlement at 533–555 then allocates the full pot to sponsor and treasury, and 375–391 permits their claims independently. Winner redemption at 309–337 invokes the prize contract; a transfer revert or failed ownership verification reverts the ticket burn, redemption flags, and any settlement in that same transaction. A prior separately completed settlement or payout is not rolled back by a later failed redemption. enableRefunds at 276–300 rejects NftWon. The source has no alternate terminal exit for a permanently refusing prize.

The condition is that an admitted prize's external behavior later prevents successful delivery. That condition is allowed by the broad untrusted-prize model; it is excluded if permanent honest transferability is a support precondition. This does not identify a defect in ERC721 authorization, EVM rollback, randomness, accounting, or the amount transferred through sponsor claims. Nor does the record establish an actual malicious deployed prize or actual sale-pot loss. It establishes an economically harmful conditional outcome and conflicting support assumptions.

The root's informational/high rating, lifecycle specialist's medium/medium rating, and accounting specialist's high/medium rating differ chiefly on the promised economic liveness boundary, not on the traced control flow. The accounting specialist's high rating describes the possible magnitude of disappointed buyers' economic exposure; it does not by itself establish a stronger contract guarantee or a general unauthorized withdrawal capability. Preserve this disagreement. Root wording that no concrete hostile implementation is present is a valid execution/evidence limit, but should not imply that source review must construct such an implementation before acknowledging a conditional hostile-call path.

Defensive property for design review: every admitted prize must have an explicit support boundary and terminal-failure policy. If buyers are promised an economically usable outcome despite permanent asset noncooperation, that outcome must remain satisfiable after every permitted sponsor/treasury release. Successful initial ownerOf and callback checks do not establish that property. If honest permanent transferability is instead a precondition, state that restriction consistently; the present untrusted-prize wording overstates support. This adjudication does not prescribe changed economics or a patch.

## L-02: concrete refinement and impact limits

RaffleFactory.sol 163–168 rejects sponsorRecipient when its current registry entry or a fixed forbidden-address comparison matches. Raffle.sol 193–199 repeats the destination screen and stores the recipient. Raffle.sol 593–604 queries the same factory's registry only for an address with code, so a distinct code-less, unregistered future clone address can pass. RaffleFactory.sol 105–125 later deploys clones and registers them; the pinned Clones.sol 32–33 and 47–60 use CREATE. No calculation or deployment was performed here, and this conclusion does not assume CREATE2. The condition is simply that the sponsor-configured address later becomes a successfully registered distinct same-factory clone before return of the first prize.

Raffle.sol 395–406 permits anyone to release the prize in CashWon or Refunding. It sets prizeClaimed, calls raw transferFrom to the immutable sponsorRecipient, and checks only the resulting owner. It does not call the current destination screen. In contrast, quote release calls _transferQuote at 564–566, which does screen the current destination. Pinned ERC721.sol 115–135 distinguishes raw transferFrom from safeTransferFrom: only the latter invokes receiver acceptance. Thus the destination clone's receiver restrictions at Raffle.sol 421–425 are not a barrier to an ordinary raw NFT transfer. Successful ownership verification confirms ownership by the forbidden recipient; it does not prove that the recipient can recover the asset.

The received NFT is distinct from the destination clone's configured prize. The inspected Raffle implementation has no generic external-asset recovery route, and its explicit receiver documentation at 409–411 recognizes this condition. Its sponsor/winner prize exits use only its configured prizeToken and prizeTokenId. The first raffle can therefore complete its sponsor-prize return while transferring the unrelated NFT into a known second clone and marking its own prize released. The absence of an arbitrary-asset recovery method is consistent with the immutable architecture; the specific issue is allowing this protocol-controlled release despite the explicit isolation requirement.

This is sponsor misconfiguration or intentional self-directed loss, not a demonstrated mechanism to replace another user's immutable recipient. Permissionless release affects who can trigger the already configured transfer, not who chooses its recipient. The source does not show an adversary deploying a factory clone over an ordinary controlled EOA, redirecting a correctly configured sponsor's prize, stealing the NFT, or affecting the second raffle's accounted cash. These limits support low severity. If sponsor-recipient misuse is explicitly excluded from the isolation promise, informational would also be defensible; the supplied categorical no-assets-between-clones requirement does not provide that exclusion.

Defensive property: immediately before any protocol-controlled prize delivery, reject a destination that is then prohibited under the intended isolation scope. Specify the terminal behavior for immutable recipients that become invalid after initialization. Rechecking alone would preserve isolation but leave the original raffle unable to deliver to its immutable recipient; it is not a complete recovery policy. No second generic cross-factory finding is needed for this same-factory example.
