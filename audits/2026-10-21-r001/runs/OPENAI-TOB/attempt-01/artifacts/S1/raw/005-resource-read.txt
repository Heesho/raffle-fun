FILE audit/resources/plugins__audit-context-building__agents__function-analyzer.md
     1	---
     2	name: function-analyzer
     3	description: "Analyzes one function in depth for audit context: invariants, assumptions, and what its callees establish. Writes the prose analysis to disk and returns a compact record. Use for dense functions, data-flow chains, cryptographic code, and state machines."
     4	tools: Read, Grep, Glob, Write
     5	---
     6	
     7	You analyze one function at a time and produce understanding, not conclusions. Your output feeds a later
     8	vulnerability-hunting phase that has not run yet.
     9	
    10	Structure, invariants, and assumptions are in scope. Vulnerabilities, fixes, exploits, and severity ratings
    11	are not. An assumption that nothing enforces is recorded as an unenforced assumption, with the line that
    12	should have enforced it — the hunting phase decides whether it matters. If you find yourself writing
    13	"vulnerability", "exploit", or "severity", the observation underneath is usually still worth keeping;
    14	restate it as the structural fact it rests on.
    15	
    16	## What you produce
    17	
    18	Two things, and they are not the same document:
    19	
    20	1. **The prose analysis**, written to the path you are given with the Write tool. This is the deliverable and
    21	   it should be thorough. Follow `{baseDir}/skills/audit-context-building/resources/ANALYSIS_FORMAT.md`.
    22	2. **The structured record** you return. A compact index into the prose — the invariants, the assumptions and
    23	   what establishes each, the callees and what the caller depends on them for, and the open questions. It
    24	   exists so the orchestrator never has to load the prose. Do not summarize the prose into it; it holds
    25	   different, shorter content.
    26	
    27	## Read the callees
    28	
    29	This is the part that distinguishes a real analysis from a plausible one.
    30	
    31	A caller's correctness usually rests on something a callee establishes. From the caller alone that dependency
    32	is invisible: a bound looks enforced because the value came back from a function whose name implies a check.
    33	
    34	So when the callee's source is available — internal or external, it makes no difference — read it, and record
    35	what the caller depends on it to establish. Walk every path through the callee, not only the one that returns
    36	successfully. A precondition established on three paths out of four is an assumption, not an invariant, and
    37	the fourth path is the interesting one. An output parameter left unwritten on an early return, a check that
    38	sits behind a conditional, a loop that can exit before it validates: these are what you are looking for.
    39	
    40	When source is not available, the callee is adversarial. Record what is sent to it, what is assumed about it,
    41	and the outcomes you have not excluded: failure, a hostile return value, an unexpected state change, re-entry
    42	into your caller before its own writes land.
    43	
    44	For every assumption, name where it is established. When nothing establishes it, write "nothing found" —
    45	that is a finding for the next phase, and it is the single most valuable thing you produce.
    46	
    47	## Grounding
    48	
    49	Cite a line for every structural claim. If you cannot point at one, do not assert it — put it in open
    50	questions as "unclear; need to inspect X". Never infer behavior from a name: a function called
    51	`validate_length` may not validate anything. When new evidence contradicts something you wrote earlier,
    52	correct it in place and say what changed.
    53	
    54	No hedge words. "Probably", "seems to", and "should be" each resolve to either a cited claim or an open
    55	question.
    56	
    57	Depth follows the code. Branches, external calls, and state mutations earn analysis; a three-line block that
    58	copies a value earns three lines. There is no minimum count of invariants or assumptions — a short record
    59	whose claims each cite a line is worth more than a long one padded to fill a template. Returning few
    60	invariants because the function has few is correct. Returning open questions is a complete analysis;
    61	leaving them unwritten is not.
    62	
    63	## Reference

     2	
     3	The output format for a per-function analysis. The skill body defines what to analyze; this defines how to
     4	write it down.
     5	
     6	## Structure
     7	
     8	One document per function, sections in this order, separated by `---`:
     9	
    10	```markdown
    11	## `functionName` in path/to/file.ext (L40-L88)
    12	
    13	**Purpose:** Its role in the system and what breaks without it.
    14	
    15	**Inputs & Assumptions:**
    16	- `param` (type): what it is. Trust: untrusted | semi-trusted | trusted.
    17	- Implicit: state read, caller identity, environment, clock.
    18	- Preconditions: what must hold on entry, and what establishes each.
    19	
    20	**Outputs & Effects:**
    21	- Returns, state writes, events or messages, external interactions, postconditions.
    22	
    23	**Block-by-Block:**
    24	
    25	​```language
    26	// L52-L54
    27	<the code>
    28	​```
    29	- **What:** one sentence.
    30	- **Why here:** what its position in the order buys.
    31	- **Assumes:** what must hold for it to be correct.
    32	- **Establishes:** the invariant it creates, if any.
    33	- **Depended on by:** the later logic that rests on it.
    34	
    35	**Cross-Function Dependencies:**
    36	- Callee `name` (internal | external-source-available | external-black-box): what this function depends on it
    37	  to establish, and on which paths.
    38	- Callers: who reaches this, and what they assume it enforces.
    39	- Shared state: which other functions touch it.
    40	- Invariant couplings: how this function's invariants interact with the system's.
    41	
    42	**Open Questions:**
    43	- unclear; need to inspect X
    44	```
    45	
    46	## Conventions
    47	
    48	Cite lines as `L45` or `L98-L102`. Label code blocks with the language. For every assumption, say what makes
    49	it true — and write `nothing found` when nothing does.
    50	
    51	Spend words where the code earns them. Branches, calls out, and anything that changes stored data deserve
    52	real attention; three lines that copy a value deserve three lines back. There is no minimum number of
    53	anything. Padding a section to hit a count produces text that looks like analysis and isn't.
    54	
    55	Leave a section out only when it is genuinely empty, and say so: "No external calls." A missing section could
    56	mean "none" or "never checked", and the reader cannot tell which.
    57	
    58	## Before you finish
    59	
    60	Check that every claim either cites a line or sits in Open Questions. Check that you followed every path
    61	through each function called, not just the one that succeeds. If something you wrote earlier turns out to be
    62	wrong, fix it where it stands and say what changed.
    63	
    64	Cut the hedges. "Probably", "seems to", and "should be" each become either a claim with a line number or an
    65	open question.
    66	
    67	Finishing with open questions is a complete analysis. Finishing with open questions you never wrote down is
    68	not.

     1	# Worked Example
     2	
     3	A complete per-function analysis. The subject is C; the format is language-neutral, and the notes at the end
     4	cover what changes for contract code.
     5	
     6	The point of this example is the callee. `session_acquire` looks safe read on its own, and the analysis only
     7	becomes accurate once `session_lookup` has been read.
     8	
     9	---
    10	
    11	## `session_acquire` in src/session.c (L112-L138)
    12	
    13	```c
    14	// L112
    15	int session_acquire(uint32_t id, struct session **out) {
    16	    struct session *s = session_lookup(id);      // L113
    17	    if (!s)                                       // L114
    18	        return -ENOENT;
    19	    s->refcount++;                                // L116
    20	    *out = s;                                     // L117
    21	    return 0;
    22	}
    23	```
    24	
    25	**Purpose:** Hands a caller a borrowed reference to a live session and records that the borrow happened, so
    26	the session is not freed underneath it. Every caller that later calls `session_release` is paired with this
    27	function; the refcount discipline of the whole session table rests on that pairing.
    28	
    29	**Inputs & Assumptions:**
    30	- `id` (uint32_t): session identifier. Trust: **untrusted** — reaches this from the request parser at
    31	  `src/proto.c:L88` without validation.
    32	- `out` (struct session **): caller-provided storage for the result. Trust: trusted (internal callers only,
    33	  per the caller list below).
    34	- Implicit: the global session table `g_sessions` (`src/session.c:L31`) and the lock protecting it.
    35	- Precondition: `out` is non-NULL. Nothing in this function establishes it and nothing checks it; both
    36	  callers pass the address of a local (`src/proto.c:L94`, `src/admin.c:L51`).
    37	- Precondition: the caller holds `g_session_lock`. Established by the callers, not here — see Open Questions.
    38	
    39	**Outputs & Effects:**
    40	- Returns `0` on success, `-ENOENT` when no session matches.
    41	- State write: increments `s->refcount` (L116).
    42	- State write: `*out` on the success path only. **On the error path `*out` is left untouched** (L114), so a
    43	  caller that does not check the return value reads whatever was in its local.
    44	- No external interactions, no events.
    45	- Postcondition on success: the session's refcount is one higher and the caller owes a matching
    46	  `session_release`.
    47	
    48	**Block-by-Block:**
    49	
    50	```c
    51	// L113
    52	struct session *s = session_lookup(id);
    53	```
    54	- **What:** Resolves the untrusted `id` to a session pointer.
    55	- **Why here:** Nothing can proceed without the lookup, and it is the only place `id` is consumed.
    56	- **Assumes:** `session_lookup` returns NULL rather than a stale pointer for an expired session.
    57	- **Establishes:** nothing on its own — see the callee analysis below, which is where this gets interesting.
    58	- **Depended on by:** L114 and L116 both.
    59	
    60	```c
    61	// L114-L115
    62	if (!s)
    63	    return -ENOENT;
    64	```
    65	- **What:** Rejects a lookup miss.
    66	- **Why here:** Guards the dereference at L116.
    67	- **Assumes:** NULL is the only failure representation `session_lookup` uses.
    68	- **Establishes:** `s != NULL` for the remainder of the function.
    69	- **Depended on by:** L116, L117.
    70	
    71	```c
    72	// L116-L117
    73	s->refcount++;
    74	*out = s;
    75	```
    76	- **What:** Records the borrow and publishes the pointer.
    77	- **Why here:** After the NULL guard; the increment precedes publication so the caller never holds a pointer
    78	  that has not been counted.
    79	- **Assumes:** the caller holds `g_session_lock`, since `refcount++` is not atomic (`refcount` is a plain
    80	  `int` at `src/session.h:L22`).
    81	- **Establishes:** the borrow is accounted for.
    82	- **Depended on by:** every subsequent `session_release`.
    83	
    84	**Cross-Function Dependencies:**
    85	
    86	- **Callee `session_lookup` (internal, src/session.c:L94-L110):** read in full. It walks `g_sessions` and
    87	  returns the matching entry. Two properties matter here and neither is visible from `session_acquire`:
    88	  1. It compares `s->id == id` (L102) but **does not check `s->state`**. An entry in state
    89	     `SESSION_CLOSING` (set at L204 of `session_close`, which does not remove the entry from the table until
    90	     L211) still matches. So `session_acquire` can take a reference to a session that is mid-teardown.
    91	     `session_acquire` reads as though liveness were established; it is established nowhere.
    92	  2. It returns NULL only when the walk falls off the end (L109). The expiry check at L104 `continue`s past
    93	     expired entries rather than deleting them, so expiry is enforced only on the lookup path — an entry
    94	     already borrowed by another caller stays reachable through that caller's pointer.
    95	- **Callers:** `proto_handle_request` (`src/proto.c:L94`) — takes `g_session_lock` at L91, so the locking
    96	  precondition holds. `admin_dump_session` (`src/admin.c:L51`) — **no lock acquisition anywhere in the
    97	  function**; the increment at L116 races. That path assumes a precondition nothing establishes.
    98	- **Shared state:** `g_sessions` with `session_lookup`, `session_close`, `session_reap`. `s->refcount` with
    99	  `session_release`.
   100	- **Invariant coupling:** the table's central invariant — every entry's refcount equals the number of
   101	  outstanding borrows — holds only if every acquire is under the lock. One of the two callers breaks that.
   102	
   103	**Open Questions:**
   104	- unclear; need to inspect whether `g_session_lock` is documented as a caller precondition anywhere. Nothing
   105	  in `session.h` states it, and only one of two callers honors it.
   106	- unclear; need to inspect `session_reap` (`src/reap.c`) to know whether it can free an entry whose refcount
   107	  is non-zero. That decides whether the `SESSION_CLOSING` window above is reachable in practice.
   108	
   109	---
   110	
   111	## What this example shows
   112	
   113	The `SESSION_CLOSING` observation and the unlocked-caller observation are both invisible from
   114	`session_acquire` alone. Reading only the subject function, `session_lookup` returning non-NULL reads as
   115	though it established liveness. Reading the callee shows it establishes identity and nothing more.
   116	
   117	Both are recorded as unenforced assumptions with the line that should have enforced them. Neither is called a
   118	use-after-free or a race condition, neither gets a severity, and no fix is proposed — the hunting phase makes
   119	those calls with the whole system model in front of it.
   120	
   121	Note also what the record does *not* do: there is no invariant invented to reach a count, and blocks that do
   122	one thing get one line each.

   126	## The same shape in Solidity
   127	
   128	The format does not change between domains; see [DOMAIN_NOTES.md](DOMAIN_NOTES.md) for the full mapping. This
   129	abbreviated record shows the contract idioms in place, and the same callee trap in a different dialect.
   130	
   131	```solidity
   132	// L61
   133	function withdraw(uint256 amount) external {
   134	    require(_debit(msg.sender, amount), "insufficient");   // L62
   135	    (bool ok, ) = msg.sender.call{value: amount}("");      // L63
   136	    require(ok, "transfer failed");                        // L64
   137	    totalDeposits -= amount;                               // L65
   138	}
   139	```
   140	
   141	**Inputs & Assumptions:**
   142	- `amount` (uint256): user-specified. Trust: **untrusted**.
   143	- Implicit: `msg.sender`, `balanceOf[msg.sender]` (L12), `hasCreditLine[msg.sender]` (L13).
   144	- Precondition: the caller's balance covers `amount`. **Established by `_debit`, and only on one of its two
   145	  paths** — see below.
   146	
   147	**Outputs & Effects:**
   148	- Storage writes: `balanceOf[msg.sender]` inside `_debit` (L48, L52); `totalDeposits` at L65.
   149	- External interaction: `msg.sender.call{value: amount}` (L63) — value transfer to an arbitrary address.
   150	- No event emitted on withdrawal. Off-chain accounting cannot observe this path.
   151	
   152	**Block-by-Block (L62-L65):**
   153	- **What:** debits the balance, sends the ether, then decrements the running total.
   154	- **Why here:** the debit precedes the transfer, which reads as checks-effects-interactions.
   155	- **Assumes:** `_debit` returning true means the balance covered the amount.
   156	- **Establishes:** nothing the later lines can rely on — `totalDeposits` at L65 lands *after* the external
   157	  call at L63, so it is not in effect during re-entry.
   158	
   159	**Cross-Function Dependencies:**
   160	- **Callee `_debit` (internal, L44-L54):** read in full. Two paths, and they do not agree.
   161	  - Default path (L50-L52): `if (balanceOf[account] < amount) return false;` then subtracts. The bound holds.
   162	  - Credit-line path (L46-L49): when `hasCreditLine[account]` is set, it subtracts inside an `unchecked`
   163	    block and returns true **without comparing balance to amount**. For those accounts the caller's
   164	    precondition is established by nothing, and the subtraction wraps instead of reverting.
   165	- **Invariant coupling:** `sum(balanceOf) == totalDeposits` is the contract's central invariant. It is
   166	  maintained on the default path and not on the credit-line path.
   167	
   168	**Open Questions:**
   169	- unclear; need to inspect who can set `hasCreditLine` and whether it is ever cleared (`L13` declares it;
   170	  no setter appears in this file).
   171	
   172	Note what the record does *not* say: it does not call L63 a reentrancy vulnerability or the `unchecked` block
   173	an integer underflow, and it proposes no fix. It says where each precondition is established, and names the
   174	one that is established nowhere. The hunting phase takes it from there.

     1	# Domain Notes
     2	
     3	The format never changes. Whatever the target, you are always asking the same four questions up front —
     4	what are the pieces, how does the outside world get in, who is on the other side, and what data survives
     5	between calls — and then, per function, what must be true, what it takes on faith, and what it calls.
     6	
     7	What changes is the answers, and what counts as a call you cannot see inside. That is what this file maps.
     8	
     9	| | ways in | who's on the other side | data that sticks around | calls you can't see inside |
    10	| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    11	| Smart contracts | `external` / `public` function | caller, owner, relayer, oracle, other protocols | storage slots | an address whose code isn't in the project |
    12	| C / C++ source | exported function, parser, syscall or IPC handler | remote peer, local user, other threads | globals, statics, long-lived heap | a linked library shipped without source |
    13	| Decompiled / firmware | task entry, interrupt handler, protocol handler | radio peer, LAN host, serial console | fixed addresses, NVRAM, DMA regions | a symbol the decompiler never resolved |
    14	| Web services (Go, Rust, Python) | route handler, RPC method, queue consumer | logged-in user, anonymous caller, internal service | database rows, cache entries, sessions | a third-party API |
    15	
    16	## Contracts
    17	
    18	The original target of this plugin, and the one where "external call" carries the most weight. A call to an
    19	address whose code is not in scope is the black-box case: record the value and calldata sent, and the
    20	outcomes not excluded — revert, a hostile return value, and re-entry into the caller before its own state
    21	writes land. Whether the write lands before or after the call is usually the whole question in "why here".
    22	
    23	Implicit inputs are larger than they look: the caller identity, the block timestamp, the gas left, and
    24	anything read from another contract. Under effects, storage writes and emitted events are separate lines,
    25	because indexers depend on the events and solvency depends on the writes.
    26	
    27	Watch for `unchecked` blocks and assembly. Both suspend guarantees the surrounding code is written as though
    28	it still has, which is exactly the shape the continuity rule exists to catch — a caller relies on a checked
    29	subtraction that a callee performs unchecked.
    30	
    31	## C and C++ source
    32	
    33	Bounds, lifetimes, and integer width carry most of the invariants. An out-parameter is the classic continuity
    34	trap: the caller checks the return code and uses the out-parameter, and one path through the callee returns
    35	success without writing it, or writes it without bounding it.
    36	
    37	Record who owns each pointer and until when. `free` on one path and not another, a borrow that outlives the
    38	lock that protected it, and a length in `int` compared against a `size_t` are all structural facts, not
    39	findings. Note which calls are behind `#ifdef` — a path that only exists in one build configuration is still
    40	a path, and orientation should say which configuration was read.
    41	
    42	## Decompiled binaries and firmware
    43	
    44	Three things differ, and they all land in orientation.
    45	
    46	**Function boundaries are themselves a finding.** Before anything can be ranked, the functions have to be
    47	recovered, and the recovery is fallible. Say which entry points were identified, how, and what was left
    48	unattributed. `FUN_80104a2c` is a name that tells you nothing — the rule against inferring behavior from a
    49	name is not a caution here, it is the default condition.
    50	
    51	**Go top-down from task entry points.** Starting at RTOS task entries, interrupt handlers, and protocol
    52	handlers and working downward beats exploring outward from whatever looked interesting. The entrypoint list
    53	is the work queue.
    54	
    55	**Most callees are black boxes, and that is the normal case rather than an exception.** A call to an address
    56	with no recovered body — a ROM thunk, a syscall, a library the decompiler did not resolve — gets the same
    57	treatment as an unknown external contract: what is passed, what is assumed, what is not excluded. A bound
    58	that is never established in the visible listing is an assumption with `establishedBy: "nothing found"`, and
    59	that is a complete and useful answer, not a failure to finish.
    60	
    61	Keep a coverage record alongside the dossier: which tasks and handlers have been analyzed and which have not.
    62	Without it there is no way to tell a clean subsystem from an unread one.
    63	
    64	## Services
    65	
    66	Logic and authorization carry more weight than memory safety. The trust boundary is usually a middleware
    67	chain, so the continuity rule points at the framework: a handler is safe because something upstream
    68	authenticated the request, and that something is a decorator, a route registration, or a config file rather
    69	than a call in the handler's body. Record where the check actually lives, and whether every route registered
    70	on that path gets it.
    71	
    72	Concurrency is persistent state. Two handlers that read-modify-write the same row without a transaction are
    73	coupled even though neither calls the other; that belongs in shared state and invariant couplings.

   240	    function requestDraw() external payable override nonReentrant returns (uint256 requestId) {
   241	        _requireStatus(Status.Active);
   242	        if (block.timestamp < endTime) revert RaffleNotEnded(endTime, block.timestamp);
   243	        if (totalEntries == 0) revert NoEntriesSold();
   244	        uint256 requestDeadline = drawRequestDeadline();
   245	        if (block.timestamp >= requestDeadline) {
   246	            revert DrawRequestWindowExpired(requestDeadline, block.timestamp);
   247	        }
   248	
   249	        uint256 quotedFee = getVrfRequestPrice();
   250	        if (msg.value < quotedFee) revert InsufficientVrfFee(quotedFee, msg.value);
   251	
   252	        status = Status.Drawing;
   253	        drawRequestedAt = uint64(block.timestamp);
   254	        _requestInFlight = true;
   255	        bytes memory extraArgs = VRFV2PlusClient._argsToBytes(VRFV2PlusClient.ExtraArgsV1({ nativePayment: true }));
   256	        uint256 paidPrice;
   257	        (requestId, paidPrice) = requestRandomnessPayInNative(callbackGasLimit, requestConfirmations, 1, extraArgs);
   258	        if (paidPrice > msg.value) revert InsufficientVrfFee(paidPrice, msg.value);
   259	        vrfRequestId = requestId;
   260	        _requestInFlight = false;
   261	
   262	        uint256 excess = msg.value - paidPrice;
   263	        if (excess != 0) {
   264	            bool success;
   265	            address refundRecipient = msg.sender;
   266	            assembly ("memory-safe") {
   267	                success := call(gas(), refundRecipient, excess, 0, 0, 0, 0)
   268	            }
   269	            if (!success) revert NativeRefundFailed(msg.sender, excess);
   270	        }
   271	
   272	        emit DrawRequested(requestId, msg.sender, paidPrice, excess, drawRequestedAt, callbackDeadline());
   273	    }

   309	    function redeemWinningTicket(uint256 ticketId) external override nonReentrant returns (uint256 cashAmount) {
   310	        if (winnerRedeemed) revert WinningTicketAlreadyRedeemed();

   312	        if (result != Status.NftWon && result != Status.CashWon) revert InvalidStatus(result);

   321	        if (msg.sender != winner) revert NotTicketOwner(ticketId, msg.sender, winner);
   322

   324	        winnerRedeemed = true;

   349	    function refundTickets(uint256[] calldata ticketIds) external override nonReentrant returns (uint256 amount) {
   350	        _requireStatus(Status.Refunding);
   351	        uint256 ticketQuantity = ticketIds.length;
   352	        if (ticketQuantity == 0 || ticketQuantity > RaffleConstants.MAX_REFUND_TICKET_BATCH_SIZE) {
   353	            revert InvalidTicketBatchSize(ticketQuantity, RaffleConstants.MAX_REFUND_TICKET_BATCH_SIZE);
   354	        }
   355	
   356	        uint256 aggregateEntries = 0;
   357	        for (uint256 index; index < ticketQuantity; ++index) {

   359	            address owner = ownerOf(ticketId);
   360	            if (msg.sender != owner) revert NotTicketOwner(ticketId, msg.sender, owner);
   361

   363	            aggregateEntries += uint256(lastEntry) - uint256(firstEntry) + 1;

   504	    /// @dev Authenticated randomness selects only an entry. No ticket search, loop, user call, or token transfer
   505	    ///      occurs. In-flight, late, wrong-request, duplicate, and ABI-decodable wrong-length callbacks are ignored for
   506	    ///      liveness; malformed ABI calldata reverts during Solidity decoding before this handler runs.
   507	    /// @param requestId Wrapper-authenticated request identifier supplied by the callback.
   508	    /// @param randomWords Callback words; exactly one word is required for resolution.
   509	    function fulfillRandomWords(uint256 requestId, uint256[] memory randomWords) internal override {
   510	        if (
   511	            _requestInFlight || status != Status.Drawing || requestId != vrfRequestId || randomWords.length != 1
   512	                || block.timestamp >= callbackDeadline()
   513	        ) {
   514	            emit VrfCallbackIgnored(requestId, vrfRequestId, status);
   515	            return;
   516	        }
   517	
   518	        uint128 resolvedEntry = uint128((randomWords[0] % uint256(totalEntries)) + 1);
   519	        winningEntry = resolvedEntry;
   520	        resolvedAt = uint64(block.timestamp);
   521	        status = totalEntries >= reserveEntries ? Status.NftWon : Status.CashWon;
   522	
   523	        emit RaffleResolved(requestId, resolvedEntry, status);
   524	    }

   528	    /// @dev Validates the winning range once and allocates all terminal quote-token liabilities without transferring
   529	    ///      an asset or reading ticket ownership. Both percentage allocations round down; sponsor proceeds are the
   530	    ///      remainder.
   531	    /// @param ticketId Ticket whose inclusive range must contain the selected winning entry.
   532	    /// @return cashAmount Raw six-decimal quote-token units allocated to the cash winner, or zero for `NftWon`.
   533	    function _settleWinningTicket(uint256 ticketId) private returns (uint256 cashAmount) {
   534	        if (settlementComplete) revert SettlementAlreadyComplete();
   535	        Status result = status;
   536	        if (result != Status.NftWon && result != Status.CashWon) revert InvalidStatus(result);
   537	
   538	        _requireWinningTicket(ticketId);
   539	        uint256 grossPot = unsettledPot;
   540	        uint256 protocolFee = Math.mulDiv(grossPot, RaffleConstants.PROTOCOL_FEE_BPS, RaffleConstants.BPS);
   541	        uint256 sponsorAmount;
   542	
   543	        if (result == Status.NftWon) {
   544	            sponsorAmount = grossPot - protocolFee;
   545	        } else {
   546	            cashAmount = Math.mulDiv(grossPot, RaffleConstants.CASH_WINNER_BPS, RaffleConstants.BPS);
   547	            sponsorAmount = grossPot - protocolFee - cashAmount;
   548	            winnerProceeds = cashAmount;
   549	        }
   550	
   551	        settlementComplete = true;
   552	        winningTicketId = ticketId;
   553	        unsettledPot = 0;
   554	        sponsorProceeds = sponsorAmount;
   555	        protocolFees = protocolFee;
   556	
   557	        emit WinningTicketSettled(ticketId, msg.sender, result, cashAmount, protocolFee, sponsorAmount);
   558	    }

   588	    ///      Initialization supplies the prospective prize token because clone storage is not populated yet; runtime
   589	    ///      checks pass the stored prize token. Code-less addresses are not queried in the registry.
   590	    /// @param destination Address being screened.
   591	    /// @param prizeToken_ Configured or prospective prize-token address to reject.
   592	    /// @return unsafeDestination True when the address is known same-factory protocol plumbing.
   593	    function _isProtocolDestination(address destination, address prizeToken_)
   594	        private
   595	        view
   596	        returns (bool unsafeDestination)
   597	    {
   598	        IRaffleFactory canonicalFactory = IRaffleFactory(factory);
   599	        unsafeDestination = destination == address(this) || destination == factory || destination == address(quoteToken)
   600	            || destination == address(i_vrfV2PlusWrapper) || destination == prizeToken_
   601	            || destination == canonicalFactory.raffleImplementation();
   602	        if (!unsafeDestination && destination.code.length != 0) {
   603	            unsafeDestination = canonicalFactory.isRaffle(destination);
   604	        }

    97	    function createRaffle(CreateRaffleParams calldata params) external override nonReentrant returns (address raffle) {
    98	        _validateCreateParams(params);
    99	        uint256 currentTime = block.timestamp;
   100	        if (params.endTime <= currentTime) revert InvalidEndTime(currentTime, params.endTime);
   101	        uint256 saleDuration = uint256(params.endTime) - currentTime;
   102	        if (saleDuration > RaffleConstants.MAX_SALE_DURATION) {
   103	            revert SaleDurationTooLong(saleDuration, RaffleConstants.MAX_SALE_DURATION);
   104	        }
   105	        uint256 raffleId = ++raffleCount;
   106	        raffle = Clones.clone(raffleImplementation);
   107	        // The fresh fixed-target clone can initialize only once; this function is nonReentrant and atomic.
   108	        // slither-disable-next-line reentrancy-no-eth
   109	        IRaffle(raffle)
   110	            .initialize(
   111	                IRaffle.RaffleInitParams({
   112	                sponsor: msg.sender,
   113	                sponsorRecipient: params.sponsorRecipient,
   114	                protocolTreasury: protocolTreasury,
   115	                prizeToken: params.prizeToken,
   116	                prizeTokenId: params.prizeTokenId,
   117	                raffleId: raffleId,
   118	                reserveEntries: params.reserveEntries,
   119	                endTime: params.endTime
   120	            })
   121	            );
   122	
   123	        raffleById[raffleId] = raffle;
   124	        idByRaffle[raffle] = raffleId;
   125	        isRaffle[raffle] = true;
   126	
   127	        IERC721 prize = IERC721(params.prizeToken);
   128	        prize.safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, raffle, params.prizeTokenId);
   129	        if (prize.ownerOf(params.prizeTokenId) != raffle) {
   130	            revert PrizeEscrowVerificationFailed(raffle, params.prizeToken, params.prizeTokenId);
   131	        }
   132	        if (IRaffle(raffle).status() != IRaffle.Status.Active) {
   133	            revert PrizeEscrowVerificationFailed(raffle, params.prizeToken, params.prizeTokenId);
   134	        }
   135	
   136	        emit RaffleCreated(
   137	            raffleId,
   138	            raffle,
   139	            msg.sender,
   140	            params.sponsorRecipient,
   141	            params.prizeToken,
   142	            params.prizeTokenId,
   143	            quoteToken,
   144	            protocolTreasury,
   145	            params.reserveEntries,
   146	            params.endTime
   147	        );

   153	    /// @param params Prospective raffle configuration supplied by the sponsor.
   154	    function _validateCreateParams(CreateRaffleParams calldata params) private view {
   155	        _requireContract(params.prizeToken);
   156	        if (params.sponsorRecipient == address(0)) revert ZeroAddress();
   157	        if (
   158	            params.prizeToken == address(this) || params.prizeToken == quoteToken || params.prizeToken == vrfWrapper
   159	                || params.prizeToken == raffleImplementation || isRaffle[params.prizeToken]
   160	        ) {
   161	            revert UnsafeProtocolDestination(params.prizeToken);
   162	        }
   163	        if (
   164	            params.sponsorRecipient == address(this) || params.sponsorRecipient == quoteToken
   165	                || params.sponsorRecipient == vrfWrapper || params.sponsorRecipient == params.prizeToken
   166	                || params.sponsorRecipient == raffleImplementation || isRaffle[params.sponsorRecipient]
   167	        ) {
   168	            revert UnsafeProtocolDestination(params.sponsorRecipient);
   169	        }
   170	
   171	        bool supports721 = false;
   172	        try IERC165(params.prizeToken).supportsInterface(type(IERC721).interfaceId) returns (bool supported) {
   173	            supports721 = supported;
   174	        } catch {
   175	            revert UnsupportedPrizeToken(params.prizeToken);
   176	        }
   177	        if (!supports721) revert UnsupportedPrizeToken(params.prizeToken);
   178	        if (params.reserveEntries == 0) revert ZeroReserveEntries();
   179	    }

   182	    /// @param account Address required to be a contract.
   183	    function _requireContract(address account) private view {
   184	        if (account.code.length == 0) revert NotContract(account);
   185	    }

   205	    /**
   206	     * @dev Transfers `tokenId` from its current owner to `to`, or alternatively mints (or burns) if the current owner
   207	     * (or `to`) is the zero address. Returns the owner of the `tokenId` before the update.
   208	     *
   209	     * The `auth` argument is optional. If the value passed is non 0, then this function will check that
   210	     * `auth` is either the owner of the token, or approved to operate on the token (by the owner).
   211	     *
   212	     * Emits a {Transfer} event.
   213	     *
   214	     * NOTE: If overriding this function in a way that tracks balances, see also {_increaseBalance}.
   215	     */
   216	    function _update(address to, uint256 tokenId, address auth) internal virtual returns (address) {
   217	        address from = _ownerOf(tokenId);
   218	
   219	        // Perform (optional) operator check
   220	        if (auth != address(0)) {
   221	            _checkAuthorized(from, auth, tokenId);
   222	        }
   223	
   224	        // Execute the update
   225	        if (from != address(0)) {
   226	            // Clear approval. No need to re-authorize or emit the Approval event
   227	            _approve(address(0), tokenId, address(0), false);
   228	
   229	            unchecked {
   230	                _balances[from] -= 1;
   231	            }
   232	        }
   233	
   234	        if (to != address(0)) {
   235	            unchecked {
   236	                _balances[to] += 1;
   237	            }
   238	        }
   239	
   240	        _owners[tokenId] = to;
   241	
   242	        emit Transfer(from, to, tokenId);
   243	
   244	        return from;
   245	    }

   383	    function _approve(address to, uint256 tokenId, address auth, bool emitEvent) internal virtual {
   384	        // Avoid reading the owner unless necessary
   385	        if (emitEvent || auth != address(0)) {

   387	
   388	            // We do not use _isAuthorized because single-token approvals should not be able to call approve

   399	    }

   200	     * @dev Calculates floor(x * y / denominator) with full precision. Throws if result overflows a uint256 or
   201	     * denominator == 0.
   202	     *
   203	     * Original credit to Remco Bloemen under MIT license (https://xn--2-umb.com/21/muldiv) with further edits by
   204	     * Uniswap Labs also under MIT license.
   205	     */
   206	    function mulDiv(uint256 x, uint256 y, uint256 denominator) internal pure returns (uint256 result) {
   207	        unchecked {
   208	            (uint256 high, uint256 low) = mul512(x, y);
   209	
   210	            // Handle non-overflow cases, 256 by 256 division.
   211	            if (high == 0) {
   212	                // Solidity will revert if denominator == 0, unlike the div opcode on its own.
   213	                // The surrounding unchecked block does not change this fact.
   214	                // See https://docs.soliditylang.org/en/latest/control-structures.html#checked-or-unchecked-arithmetic.
   215	                return low / denominator;
   216	            }
   217	
   218	            // Make sure the result is less than 2²⁵⁶. Also prevents denominator == 0.
   219	            if (denominator <= high) {
   220	                Panic.panic(ternary(denominator == 0, Panic.DIVISION_BY_ZERO, Panic.UNDER_OVERFLOW));
   221	            }
   222	
   223	            ///////////////////////////////////////////////
   224	            // 512 by 256 division.
   225	            ///////////////////////////////////////////////
   226	
   227	            // Make division exact by subtracting the remainder from [high low].
   228	            uint256 remainder;
   229	            assembly ("memory-safe") {
   230	                // Compute remainder using mulmod.
   231	                remainder := mulmod(x, y, denominator)
   232	
   233	                // Subtract 256 bit number from 512 bit number.
   234	                high := sub(high, gt(remainder, low))
   235	                low := sub(low, remainder)
   236	            }
   237	
   238	            // Factor powers of two out of denominator and compute largest power of two divisor of denominator.
   239	            // Always >= 1. See https://cs.stackexchange.com/q/138556/92363.
   240	
   241	            uint256 twos = denominator & (0 - denominator);
   242	            assembly ("memory-safe") {
   243	                // Divide denominator by twos.
   244	                denominator := div(denominator, twos)
   245	
   246	                // Divide [high low] by twos.
   247	                low := div(low, twos)
   248	
   249	                // Flip twos such that it is 2²⁵⁶ / twos. If twos is zero, then it becomes one.
   250	                twos := add(div(sub(0, twos), twos), 1)
   251	            }
   252	
   253	            // Shift in bits from high into low.
   254	            low |= high * twos;
   255	
   256	            // Invert denominator mod 2²⁵⁶. Now that denominator is an odd number, it has an inverse modulo 2²⁵⁶ such
   257	            // that denominator * inv ≡ 1 mod 2²⁵⁶. Compute the inverse by starting with a seed that is correct for
   258	            // four bits. That is, denominator * inv ≡ 1 mod 2⁴.
   259	            uint256 inverse = (3 * denominator) ^ 2;
   260	
   261	            // Use the Newton-Raphson iteration to improve the precision. Thanks to Hensel's lifting lemma, this also
   262	            // works in modular arithmetic, doubling the correct bits in each step.
   263	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2⁸
   264	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2¹⁶
   265	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2³²
   266	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2⁶⁴
   267	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2¹²⁸
   268	            inverse *= 2 - denominator * inverse; // inverse mod 2²⁵⁶
   269	
   270	            // Because the division is now exact we can divide by multiplying with the modular inverse of denominator.
   271	            // This will give us the correct result modulo 2²⁵⁶. Since the preconditions guarantee that the outcome is
   272	            // less than 2²⁵⁶, this is the final result. We don't need to compute the high bits of the result and high
   273	            // is no longer required.
   274	            result = low * inverse;
   275	            return result;
   276	        }
   277	    }

   306	    /**
   307	     * @dev Calculate the modular multiplicative inverse of a number in Z/nZ.
   308	     *
   309	     * If n is a prime, then Z/nZ is a field. In that case all elements are inversible, except 0.
   310	     * If n is not a prime, then Z/nZ is not a field, and some elements might not be inversible.
   311	     *
   312	     * If the input value is not inversible, 0 is returned.
   313	     *
   314	     * NOTE: If you know for sure that n is (big) a prime, it may be cheaper to use Fermat's little theorem and get the
   315	     * inverse using `Math.modExp(a, n - 2, n)`. See {invModPrime}.
   316	     */
   317	    function invMod(uint256 a, uint256 n) internal pure returns (uint256) {
   318	        unchecked {
   319	            if (n == 0) return 0;
   320	
   321	            // The inverse modulo is calculated using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm (iterative version)
   322	            // Used to compute integers x and y such that: ax + ny = gcd(a, n).
   323	            // When the gcd is 1, then the inverse of a modulo n exists and it's x.
   324	            // ax + ny = 1
   325	            // ax = 1 + (-y)n
   326	            // ax ≡ 1 (mod n) # x is the inverse of a modulo n
   327	
   328	            // If the remainder is 0 the gcd is n right away.
   329	            uint256 remainder = a % n;
   330	            uint256 gcd = n;
   331	
   332	            // Therefore the initial coefficients are:
   333	            // ax + ny = gcd(a, n) = n
   334	            // 0a + 1n = n
   335	            int256 x = 0;
   336	            int256 y = 1;
   337	
   338	            while (remainder != 0) {
   339	                uint256 quotient = gcd / remainder;
   340	
   341	                (gcd, remainder) = (
   342	                    // The old remainder is the next gcd to try.
   343	                    remainder,
   344	                    // Compute the next remainder.
   345	                    // Can't overflow given that (a % gcd) * (gcd // (a % gcd)) <= gcd
   346	                    // where gcd is at most n (capped to type(uint256).max)
   347	                    gcd - remainder * quotient
   348	                );
   349	
   350	                (x, y) = (
   351	                    // Increment the coefficient of a.
   352	                    y,
   353	                    // Decrement the coefficient of n.
   354	                    // Can overflow, but the result is casted to uint256 so that the
   355	                    // next value of y is "wrapped around" to a value between 0 and n - 1.
   356	                    x - y * int256(quotient)
   357	                );
   358	            }
   359	
   360	            if (gcd != 1) return 0; // No inverse exists.
   361	            return ternary(x < 0, n - uint256(-x), uint256(x)); // Wrap the result if it's negative.
   362	        }
   363	    }

   507	            // sequence x_n that converges toward sqrt(a). For each iteration x_n, we also define the error between
   508	            // the current value as `ε_n = | x_n - sqrt(a) |`.
   509	            //
   510	            // For our first estimation, we consider `e` the smallest power of 2 which is bigger than the square root
   511	            // of the target. (i.e. `2**(e-1) ≤ sqrt(a) < 2**e`). We know that `e ≤ 128` because `(2¹²⁸)² = 2²⁵⁶` is
   512	            // bigger than any uint256.
   513	            //
   514	            // By noticing that
   515	            // `2**(e-1) ≤ sqrt(a) < 2**e → (2**(e-1))² ≤ a < (2**e)² → 2**(2*e-2) ≤ a < 2**(2*e)`
   516	            // we can deduce that `e - 1` is `log2(a) / 2`. We can thus compute `x_n = 2**(e-1)` using a method similar
   517	            // to the msb function.

   528	            }

   530	                aa >>= 32;

   539	                xn <<= 4;
   540	            }

   544	            }
   545	            if (aa >= (1 << 2)) {
   546	                xn <<= 1;
   547	            }

   550	            //
   551	            // We can refine our estimation by noticing that the middle of that interval minimizes the error.
   552	            // If we move x_n to equal 2**(e-1) + 2**(e-2), then we reduce the error to ε_n ≤ 2**(e-2).
   553	            // This is going to be our x_0 (and ε_0)

   557	            // x_{n+1} = (x_n + a / x_n) / 2

   560	            // x_{n+1}² - a = ((x_n + a / x_n) / 2)² - a

   563	            //              = (x_n⁴ + 2 * a * x_n² + a² - 4 * a * x_n²) / (4 * x_n²)

   565	            //              = (x_n² - a)² / (2 * x_n)²

   567	            //              ≥ 0
   568	            // Which proves that for all n ≥ 1, sqrt(a) ≤ x_n
   569	            //

   571	            // ε_{n+1} = | x_{n+1} - sqrt(a) |
   572	            //         = | (x_n + a / x_n) / 2 - sqrt(a) |

   574	            //         = | (x_n - sqrt(a))² / (2 * x_n) |
   575	            //         = | ε_n² / (2 * x_n) |

   584	            //     ≤ 2**(e-4.5)
   585	            //
   586	            // For the following iterations, we use the fact that, 2**(e-1) ≤ sqrt(a) ≤ x_n:
   587	            // ε_{n+1} = ε_n² / | (2 * x_n) |
   588	            //         ≤ (2**(e-k))² / (2 * 2**(e-1))

   590	            //         ≤ 2**(e-2*k)

   602	        }

   620	        // If value has upper 128 bits set, log2 result is at least 128
   621	        r = SafeCast.toUint(x > 0xffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffff) << 7;
   622	        // If upper 64 bits of 128-bit half set, add 64 to result
   623	        r |= SafeCast.toUint((x >> r) > 0xffffffffffffffff) << 6;
   624	        // If upper 32 bits of 64-bit half set, add 32 to result
   625	        r |= SafeCast.toUint((x >> r) > 0xffffffff) << 5;
   626	        // If upper 16 bits of 32-bit half set, add 16 to result
   627	        r |= SafeCast.toUint((x >> r) > 0xffff) << 4;
   628	        // If upper 8 bits of 16-bit half set, add 8 to result
   629	        r |= SafeCast.toUint((x >> r) > 0xff) << 3;
   630	        // If upper 4 bits of 8-bit half set, add 4 to result
   631	        r |= SafeCast.toUint((x >> r) > 0xf) << 2;
   632	
   633	        // Shifts value right by the current result and use it as an index into this lookup table:
   634	        //
   635	        // | x (4 bits) |  index  | table[index] = MSB position |
   636	        // |------------|---------|-----------------------------|
   637	        // |    0000    |    0    |        table[0] = 0         |
   638	        // |    0001    |    1    |        table[1] = 0         |
   639	        // |    0010    |    2    |        table[2] = 1         |
   640	        // |    0011    |    3    |        table[3] = 1         |
   641	        // |    0100    |    4    |        table[4] = 2         |
   642	        // |    0101    |    5    |        table[5] = 2         |
   643	        // |    0110    |    6    |        table[6] = 2         |
   644	        // |    0111    |    7    |        table[7] = 2         |
   645	        // |    1000    |    8    |        table[8] = 3         |
   646	        // |    1001    |    9    |        table[9] = 3         |
   647	        // |    1010    |   10    |        table[10] = 3        |
   648	        // |    1011    |   11    |        table[11] = 3        |
   649	        // |    1100    |   12    |        table[12] = 3        |
   650	        // |    1101    |   13    |        table[13] = 3        |
   651	        // |    1110    |   14    |        table[14] = 3        |
   652	        // |    1111    |   15    |        table[15] = 3        |
   653	        //
   654	        // The lookup table is represented as a 32-byte value with the MSB positions for 0-15 in the first 16 bytes (most significant half).
   655	        assembly ("memory-safe") {

   657	        }

    24	 */

    26	library Panic {
    27	    /// @dev generic / unspecified error
    28	    uint256 internal constant GENERIC = 0x00;
    29	    /// @dev used by the assert() builtin
    30	    uint256 internal constant ASSERT = 0x01;
    31	    /// @dev arithmetic underflow or overflow
    32	    uint256 internal constant UNDER_OVERFLOW = 0x11;
    33	    /// @dev division or modulo by zero
    34	    uint256 internal constant DIVISION_BY_ZERO = 0x12;
    35	    /// @dev enum conversion error
    36	    uint256 internal constant ENUM_CONVERSION_ERROR = 0x21;
    37	    /// @dev invalid encoding in storage
    38	    uint256 internal constant STORAGE_ENCODING_ERROR = 0x22;
    39	    /// @dev empty array pop
    40	    uint256 internal constant EMPTY_ARRAY_POP = 0x31;
    41	    /// @dev array out of bounds access
    42	    uint256 internal constant ARRAY_OUT_OF_BOUNDS = 0x32;
    43	    /// @dev resource error (too large allocation or too large array)
    44	    uint256 internal constant RESOURCE_ERROR = 0x41;
    45	    /// @dev calling invalid internal function
    46	    uint256 internal constant INVALID_INTERNAL_FUNCTION = 0x51;
    47	
    48	    /// @dev Reverts with a panic code. Recommended to use with
    49	    /// the internal constants with predefined codes.
    50	    function panic(uint256 code) internal pure {
    51	        assembly ("memory-safe") {
    52	            mstore(0x00, 0x4e487b71)
    53	            mstore(0x20, code)
    54	            revert(0x1c, 0x24)
    55	        }
